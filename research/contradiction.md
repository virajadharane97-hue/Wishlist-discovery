# Where the corpus and the primary research disagree

## The contradiction

Asking another person is almost invisible in the corpus and clearly
present in the research.

In 682 relevant public comments, BLOCK_SOCIAL_VALIDATION accounts for
zero rows once the code was narrowed to its proper meaning — waiting
on a specific person's judgement. In 20 written responses, 4
respondents sent the item to a named person and reproduced the exact
message they sent, and 6 named asking a friend or family member as
somewhere they looked for information.

## Why

Public comment is addressed to an audience. The two things people
post are grievances about a purchase that already went wrong, and
questions aimed at a creator who might answer. Both are performances
with a reader in mind.

Asking your sister is neither. It happens in a private thread, it
produces no artefact anyone can scrape, and there is no reason to
announce it. "I sent the link to my sister on WhatsApp and said 'too
much for a mehendi??'" is invisible to any corpus built from public
sources, and it is the single most common resolution route in the
research.

## What it means for the analysis

The corpus is reliable for measuring what people complain about and
what they ask creators. It systematically under-measures what people
do privately. This was predicted in writing before collection began:
the project brief records that public comments would over-represent
loud post-purchase grievance and under-represent quiet pre-purchase
hesitation, and that this was the reason primary research was needed
alongside the corpus rather than instead of it.

The practical consequence is that the workaround the solution
absorbs — asking a person you consider credible — is evidenced by the
research and only indirectly by the corpus, where it appears as
external_workaround = creator on 132 of 682 rows rather than as a
blocker code.

## A second instance, same mechanism

Price behaves the same way. INTENT_PRICE_WATCH accounts for 22 of 682
corpus rows, roughly 3%, while 5 of 20 respondents gave price as
their blocker. Nobody posts "I am waiting for a sale" — they simply
wait. The behaviour generates no comment, so a corpus cannot see it.

This instance is partly confounded: BLOCK_DURABILITY_VALUE was found
to be absorbing general price complaints, so the true corpus figure
for price sensitivity is higher than 3% but was not separately
counted.
