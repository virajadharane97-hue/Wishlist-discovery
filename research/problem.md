# Problem statement

*Locked 22 August 2026.*

> Users who search outside Myntra before deciding on a saved item
> cannot resolve one specific question, because reviews report how it
> looks on arrival rather than how it performs for a body or use like
> theirs, so they scroll Instagram, ask a sibling or visit a store —
> and often still do not buy.

52 words including the em-dash clause. Exceeds the 40-word guideline;
retained because the closing clause carries evidence that no shorter
version does.

---

## Evidence by clause

*"Users who search outside Myntra before deciding on a saved item"*
16 of 20 respondents searched externally (Q13). Segment locked in
decomposition.md Section 7. Corroborated in the corpus: 132 of 682
relevant rows record asking a creator as the workaround.

*"cannot resolve one specific question"*
19 of 20 stated a specific unanswered question (Q11).
BLOCK_SIZE_SELECTION ranks first of 15 codes at 22.6% of coded rows,
with the highest severity (3.38) and highest proximity to purchase
(1.33) of any code, and ranks first under all four weighting schemes.

*"reviews report how it looks on arrival"*
8 of 20 named reviews as the source that failed them. R10: "Most
reviews are written immediately after delivery and say things like
'good quality' without explaining what that means." R11: "The reviews
mostly talk about how it looks when they receive it."

*"for a body or use like theirs"*
4 of 20 directly — R7, R12, R13, R14. See scope note below.

*"so they scroll Instagram, ask a sibling or visit a store"*
Instagram 3 of 20 (R11, R13, R18). Asked another person 4 of 20 (R8,
R9, R14, R16). Physical store 5 of 20 (R1, R4, R12, R14, R17).

*"and often still do not buy"*
R13 measured with a tape, consulted the chart, read reviews sorted by
size, followed three reviewers' advice, ordered up, and the garment
was still too big; returned and abandoned. R16 asked a friends group
and was told "meesho cargo is always shiny don't"; bought anyway and
it was tight. R14's sister replied "it's nice but where will you wear
it again". Three of the recorded workarounds produced a failure or a
discouragement. By definition all 20 still hold the item saved.

---

## Scope declaration

The root cause clause describes the largest of four patterns in the
research, covering 4 of 20 respondents directly. The other three are
adjacent and are declared out of scope for this problem statement:

*The fact is not published anywhere — 4 respondents.* R17 needs the
internal dimensions of a bag; only external dimensions are given. R18
has no size chart at all, an unanswered DM, and a brand with no
reviews anywhere. R13 needs an inseam figure. R14 needs the lining
composition. No comparable reviewer resolves these, because the
information does not exist to be found.

*The source is not credible — 3 respondents.* R16: "Meesho photos
are all clearly stolen from other websites." R8: most available
content is brand or influencer material. R9: the few customer photos
that exist are affected by lighting.

*Nobody has owned it long enough — 2 respondents.* R10 on fabric
after washing; R11 on a bag after several months of use.

All four are instances of the same structure: information exists and
does not answer the question the user holds. The statement scopes to
the largest pattern rather than describing all four vaguely.

## Drafting note

Eight versions were written. An earlier draft framed the root cause
as transferability — that the user cannot tell whether a reviewer's
experience applies to them. That framing was tested against the
responses and found to describe 4 of 20 rather than the general case,
so it was retained as the scoped root cause rather than presented as
the whole finding.

The author's own experience matches the transferability pattern. That
prior was declared in brief.md on 18 August, before data collection,
and the framing was adopted because the data supports it at 4 of 20 —
not because it was expected.
